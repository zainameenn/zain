### H2: How to use the Facebook bio generator

It takes about a minute, which is roughly how long most people stare at the Intro box before typing "Just living life".

1. **Pick the account type.** Business, creator or personal. A plumber's Page and a personal profile need very different bios.
2. **Say what you do and who it's for.** Facebook visitors often arrive checking whether you're real and local, so be specific: "plumbing in Austin" beats "home services".
3. **Add your city and one real proof point.** Years in business, a review score, a free class. One fact earns more trust than three adjectives.
4. **Preview, copy, paste.** Click Preview to see each bio in an Intro box. Pick one, copy it, then on Facebook go to the Intro section under your profile picture and edit your bio.

Already have a bio or Page intro? Switch to **Improve my current bio**, paste it in, and get five tighter versions that keep your facts.

### H2: What to put in your Facebook bio

Facebook bios get read in a very specific moment: someone found you in a group, a comment or a search, and they're deciding whether you're legit. The bio's job is to settle that in one glance.

So the order matters:

1. **What you do, in plain words.** "Family run plumbing", not "solutions for your home".
2. **Where, if you're local.** A city name tells nearby people you're for them.
3. **One reason to trust you.** A real number, a free first visit, a promise you keep.
4. **What to do next,** if there's room. Facebook has buttons for messaging and calling, so this can be short.

Calling yourself "Best plumber in town" is the bio version of Michael Scott's "World's Best Boss" mug. He bought it himself, and everyone could tell. Let the reviews say it.

### H2: Profile or Page: where everything else goes

The bio is only one part of your profile. Facebook gives other details their own fields, and using them keeps the bio short and clean.

| Profile part | What it's for | Quick tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bio (in the Intro section) | A short description of you or your Page | What you do, for whom, and where |
| Links | Links to your websites | Your shop, booking page or menu goes here, not in the bio |
| Contact info | Ways people can reach you, like phone, email and social links | Fill it in, so the bio doesn't have to list a phone number |
| Category (Pages) | Shows below your Page name in search | Pick the category your customers would search for |

Meta's help pages describe the [Intro as "a bio or brief description of yourself or your Page"](https://www.facebook.com/help/1090724464274188) and list Links, Contact info and [Category](https://www.facebook.com/help/222732947737668) as separate details. The short version: **THE BIO SAYS WHO YOU ARE, THE OTHER FIELDS SAY HOW TO REACH YOU.**

### H2: Facebook bio length, privacy and formatting

Most guides put the Facebook profile bio limit at **101 characters**, and every bio this tool writes stays under that, counting spaces, emoji and line breaks. That length also works for a Page intro, so you can use the same text on both.

A few things worth knowing before you paste:

1. **Your Intro is always public.** Meta says the information in the Intro section of your profile is always public, so leave out anything you wouldn't pin to a community noticeboard.
2. **Put links in the Links field.** A website, booking page or Instagram link belongs in Links or Contact info, where people can tap it.
3. **Short beats complete.** One clear line is easier to read on a phone than three crammed ones.
4. **Emoji are optional.** One that replaces a word, like 🔧 for a plumber, is plenty.

### H2: Facebook bio mistakes that make people scroll past

1. **A bio that could belong to anyone.** "Living my best life" tells a visitor nothing. Add one real detail.
2. **Phone numbers and opening hours in the bio.** They have their own fields, and they eat your characters.
3. **Business jargon.** "Full service solutions provider" makes people work to understand you. They won't.
4. **Claims you can't back up.** "Best", "#1" and "cheapest" invite comparison, and someone always checks.
5. **Forgetting the Page and profile are different.** If you run a business Page, write the Page bio for customers and keep your personal bio personal.
