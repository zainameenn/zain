### H2: How to use the Instagram caption generator

Bring the messy notes. The tool brings the structure, the hook and the hashtags.

1. **Say what the post is.** A photo, a carousel or a Reel, and what's in it. "Before and after of a tiny balcony" gives the tool far more to work with than "new post".
2. **Dump the details.** Prices, times, mistakes, numbers. Real details are what make a caption worth reading.
3. **Pick the goal and the vibe.** A caption built for saves reads differently from one built for comments. Add a search phrase if people would look for this topic.
4. **Preview, copy, post.** Click Preview to see each caption under a post, with the first lines showing the way they do before "more". Copy your favorite and paste it into Instagram.

Want a different take? Use **Make it shorter** or **Make it punchier** on the results, or change the vibe and run it again.

### H2: What makes a good Instagram caption

Most people see your first line, then decide in about a second whether to tap "more" or keep scrolling. So the first line carries the post.

Breaking Bad didn't open with Walter White filling in a tax form. It opened with a pair of trousers flying across the desert. Your first line needs the same energy: the surprising detail, the number, the result. Then:

1. **Add what the photo can't show.** The price, the time it took, the mistake, the reason.
2. **Use the words people search for,** naturally, in the first sentence or two.
3. **End with one ask.** Save, comment, tap the link. Pick one.

"New post! ✨" is a caption in the same way a blank postcard is a letter.

### H2: How long should an Instagram caption be?

As long as it stays interesting, and no longer. Different posts need different lengths:

| Post type | Good length | What the caption does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Photo that speaks for itself | One or two lines | Adds the one detail the photo can't show |
| Carousel or tutorial | A short paragraph plus steps | Tells people why to swipe and what they'll learn |
| Reel | One to three lines | Hooks the watch and adds a search phrase |
| Story behind the post | Several short paragraphs | Keeps people reading, line by line |
| Announcement or launch | Three to five short lines | What's new, why it matters, what to do |

Pick Short, Medium or Long in the tool and it writes to that range. Whatever the length, **THE FIRST LINE STILL HAS TO EARN THE TAP.**

### H2: Hashtags on Instagram: why 5 is now the limit

Instagram's help center now says you can use [up to 5 hashtags on a post](https://help.instagram.com/351460621611097). Any tool still handing you 30 tags is working from an old rulebook.

Five is plenty if you choose well:

1. **Go specific.** #balconygarden tells Instagram more than #nature or #love.
2. **Match the post, not the trend.** A tag only helps if your post is what people expect to find there.
3. **Put them in the caption.** Instagram's own advice on search is to put keywords and hashtags in the caption, not the comments.

The tool returns hashtags as separate chips under each caption, so you can remove any you don't like before you copy.

### H2: Keywords in captions and Instagram search

Instagram has explained that search matches what people type with usernames, bios, captions, hashtags and places, and its advice was blunt: ["put keywords and hashtags in the caption, not the comments"](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/break-down-how-instagram-search-works).

So if your post is about balcony garden ideas, say "balcony garden ideas" somewhere natural in the first couple of sentences. Add a search phrase in the tool and it does exactly that, once or twice, without stuffing.

Write for the person first. A caption that reads like a keyword list gets scrolled past, and that hurts more than a missing keyword.

### H2: Instagram caption limits and formatting

Instagram's developer documentation lists a maximum of **2,200 characters** for a caption, so every caption this tool writes stays under that, hashtags included. The counter on each result shows the exact total.

A few formatting habits worth keeping:

1. **Use short paragraphs with a blank line between them.** The tool keeps those line breaks when you copy.
2. **Front load the good stuff.** Only the first lines show before "more", so don't bury the point.
3. **Add alt text separately.** Instagram lets you [write your own alt text](https://help.instagram.com/503708446705527) for screen readers. It's a separate field, so describe the image there rather than in the caption.
4. **You can edit after posting.** Instagram lets you [edit or add a caption](https://help.instagram.com/1490745927855762) to a post you've already shared, so a typo isn't the end of the world.

### H2: Instagram caption mistakes that kill engagement

1. **Opening with "Hey guys" or "New post".** The first line is your hook. Don't spend it on a greeting.
2. **Describing what's already in the photo.** "Here's a picture of my coffee" adds nothing. Tell people what they can't see.
3. **Asking for four things at once.** Like, comment, share and save is three asks too many.
4. **A wall of generic hashtags.** Five specific tags beat twenty vague ones, and five is now the limit anyway.
5. **Copying a caption template word for word.** If it worked for a million accounts, it's invisible on yours.
